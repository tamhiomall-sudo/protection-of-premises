Why this matters

The main use decides whether a tier override applies, and in some cases who is responsible. For sites with more than one use, the Principal Use of Premises Regulations 2026 (in force from 15 October 2026) say it is the use that “relates most closely to the purpose and nature of the premises”. The factors are how the site was built or modified, whether it is still used that way, its physical layout, and how the local authority treats it. Write down why you chose it.

The one-off occasion

Will entry to it be checked? Paid entry, tickets or passes, or members and guests only.
The notification to the SIA asks for your figure and the method behind it. Write it down while you know.
About the methods

The Act gives no method for counting. The Home Office lists six in a supplementary document, which is guidance rather than law. They can give different figures for the same site and nothing says which one wins. Use the one that best reflects real peak use. A fire or licence capacity is a ceiling: where your recorded peak is reliably lower, the guidance uses the recorded peak.

Staff means employees, contractors, volunteers and anyone else working there for the public use. Giving the SIA false or misleading information is a criminal offence, so the method you declare should be one you can evidence.

    4Who is responsible

    Whoever controls the site for its public use. Owning the building or holding the licence doesn’t settle it.

    The tenanted pub correction

    In April 2026 the guidance said a brewery holding the licence for a tenanted pub was responsible. On 6 August 2026 the Home Office corrected it: the tenant controls the pub, so the tenant is responsible. Material written between those dates may still carry the error.

    Does more than one party control it? If so, each one is responsible in its own right. You can act jointly but don’t have to.
    Is that an organisation or an individual?
    Does anyone else control part of it? A landlord, a concession, a managing agent, a contractor.

      6What the Act requires

      Alongside your other duties

      The Act sits alongside the law you already work to, and doesn’t override it. These are the places they meet. Only you can settle them for your site, so record what you decide and why.

      Fire safetyYour fire strategy moves people out. The Act’s procedures may move people somewhere safer inside, or stop them entering or leaving. Decide which applies when, and who makes that call on the day.
      Physical changesNew barriers, doors, locks, glazing or entrances may need planning permission or building regulations approval, and can change your fire strategy. In a higher-risk building, the Building Safety Act 2022 adds its own approval route. Check before you commit.
      Everyone has to be able to follow themProcedures must work for disabled people and for anyone who needs help to move or to understand instructions. The Equality Act 2010 applies alongside.
      Your existing plansHealth and safety risk assessments, licence conditions and event safety plans all still apply. Join them up so staff get one set of instructions, not three.
